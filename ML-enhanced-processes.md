# ML-enhanced-processes

## Crop disease and pest detection

### Description

As I am working in an IoT company, this year there is an initiative to develop a platform that could support the functionality of crop disease and pest detection. For now, we are focusing only on the data acquisition from sensors and using some rules to produce alerts or incidents. However, once this first part is finished, I believe the next step would be to detect crop diseases and pests. In order to inform myself on this process, I did some research on it to know how it could be done and what sensors or machines we would need. I found that the most accurate models use image detection models that could determine if a crop has a disease and which one it is. This will enable the farmers to decide which sections of the plantation need to be treated and with which product.

### How and when would the process is expected to be used?

I would expect it to be used during the growing season to monitor the crops and identify signs of different diseases, including the ones developed by insects or fungi. There are different ways to collect the data that would respond to different necessities; for example, cameras mounted on drones or agricultural equipment would respond to a general examination of the plantations, while photos taken on smartphones would respond to a specific problem the farmer needs to check and have a recommendation. Sensors could also be used to monitor temperature, humidity, and gas concentration in a determinate area, and with this data, it could send an alert or signal to the farmer or the machines that a sector is probably affected by a disease and needs further investigation. The process will be especially useful for crop scouting routines and when environmental conditions make a particular disease more likely to happen. The farmers will then be in charge of investigating further when some signals are detected instead of inspecting every section of the field, using their expertise where it is more needed.

### What is this process intended to replace, and what are some benefits to the ML-enabled process?

It intends to replace manual crop scouting and visual diagnosis, in which the specialists physically inspect plants and determine if the symptoms indicate a disease or pest. The benefit will first be seen in saving time, because the scouting for large agricultural areas will be automated instead of manually inspected. The operational cost would also decrease, because now we would need fewer experts who would be in charge of confirming the diagnosis and making the action plan to follow, adding more value to the business instead of doing repetitive tasks. Another benefit is a more consistent screening, because the identification would be done by a machine and not by a human, which means the parameters to decide if a crop has a disease will not change unless the complete model is changed. Finally, it allows earlier intervention and targeted treatment, which are especially important for the business because it prevents losses of crop that would translate to economic losses.

## Traffic signal optimization

### Description

Every day I need to go outside to work or study, and I have experience that some of those times, the traffic signal is not useful for pedestrians or for drivers, especially during peak hours. I have seen traffic signals between a street and a principal avenue that normally are 1 minute each, but during peak hours, the system does a static change that gives priority to the principal avenue, granting almost 3 minutes for drivers and pedestrians to pass but only 20 seconds for the ones coming from the street. This makes sense because the principal avenue is normally more filled with people and cars; however, it causes more traffic in the street, which I have eventually seen leads to stressed drivers that makes bold movements, for example, crossing the green light at the limit to try and reach the avenue, and, as there is not one or two drivers but many that do that, most of the times that creates blockades that produce more traffic. At this moment, the solution the city has developed is sending traffic police officers to those points to help with it; however, it does not work all the time. I think this could be prevented by a system in charge of obtaining real-time information to optimize the traffic signals according to the context.

### How and when would the process is expected to be used?

It would be used at intersections or networks of intersections where traffic demand changes a lot during the day or is more directly affected by peak hours. To obtain the data, there should be cameras, connected infrastructure, and traffic sensors that could provide information about vehicle presence and traffic volume.  An ML system would use this data to determine if a green light should stay on or if it should change. Then, the current traditional method of using a static change during peak hours or sending traffic police officers to help would be replaced by a system that could respond in real time to the traffic conditions. The process would be very valuable during peak hours, unexpected congestion due to some incidents, changes between weekday and weekend traffic, or variations caused by road closures. As the application requires safety procedures to follow, these models are not currently being used, but they are being studied and simulated to respond to this universal problem present in every city.

### What is this process intended to replace, and what are some benefits to the ML-enabled process?

The process is intended to replace fixed-time traffic signals, conventional rule-based actuated signals, and police officers sent to help. Fixed-time traffic signals are based on predefined durations and historical patterns; rule-based actuated signals can react to traffic conditions but are limited by the rules; and police officers could try to help, but each one of them has different criteria and produces different outcomes, sometimes helping and sometimes worsening the situation. The proposed system would respond in real time to the change on the conditions. The principal benefit of the system would be adaptability to the traffic conditions instead of assuming that the traffic demand will follow the same pattern all day long. Also, the current solution to send police officers to help would not be necessary, and they could be present in other more important activities instead of directing traffic. It would also help with shorter vehicle queues, reduced waiting time, improved traffic flow, and reduced emissions associated with unnecessary idling.

## Detection of defects in roads and infrastructure

### Description

These last days, many countries have experienced earthquakes that have affected many buildings and infrastructure around the world. It makes me remember the earthquake in 2007 that happened in Lima; I was just a kid, and I was very frightened by it. I remember that similar images were transmitted on TV as the ones we are currently seeing on different social media: collapsed buildings, affected roads, destroyed bridges. This makes me think about the importance of detecting defects before disasters happen, so that we could ensure higher survival probabilities when a disaster appears. Among the affected infrastructure, one of the main ones that needs to be secured is the transportation infrastructure, because without it, help will not be able to reach the affected points easily in case of a disaster. Thinking about it, I checked on the process that needs to be followed when doing routine inspections of roads, bridges, tunnels, and other transportation infrastructure. Nowadays, in many countries, you have a team of professionals inspecting every area; however, that consumes time and resources that could be used in making and executing action plans instead of evaluating if an area needs reinforcement or not. The proposed ML approach is to use computer vision models to detect defects in images acquired by drones, cameras, lasers, or specialized inspection vehicles.

### How and when would the process is expected to be used?

It could be used in routine inspections, after extreme weather conditions, after disasters that may have damaged the infrastructure, and for more frequent monitoring of high-risk locations. The images and three-dimensional measurements collected by cameras, drones, lasers, and specialized inspection vehicles would be used as input to the computer vision model to determine where the defects are and what their classification is (pavement cracks, potholes, cracks on structural surfaces). The inspection vehicles with cameras and laser sensors would be used on road networks, gathering data while traveling rather than using inspectors to examine each section individually. For bridges, drones equipped with cameras are more efficient because they can access difficult areas and take images to be inspected by the model, producing information about the location and severity of the defects.

### What is this process intended to replace, and what are some benefits to the ML-enabled process?

The process intends to replace or supplement parts of the manual visual inspection and classification of infrastructure defects. As said before, traditionally, a team of inspectors is needed to examine road or structural surfaces to record visible deterioration, but with this new method, they would have a tool that automates part of the job, leaving the inspector the actual confirmation of the defects and elaboration of action plans. Then, the principal benefit would be the increase in efficiency and the saving of specialized human resources for more important tasks instead of repetitive and intensive labour that would be done by the ML model. It also enables faster inspection of large areas and more consistent identification and classification because the model will automatically process large amounts of data with the trained internal rules of the model that will not change. When using drones, it also improves worker safety and reduces the need for traffic control to inspect zones with difficult access. Another advantage is that digital inspection will create a record that can be compared over time, allowing engineers to see if a defect is increasing in severity. The final purpose of this ML model is not to eliminate the professionals from the process, but rather to help them by doing the repetitive task and provide them with better information to decide where to do a more detailed inspection or prioritize the repairs to be done.
