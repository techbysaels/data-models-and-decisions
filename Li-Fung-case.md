# Li & Fung Case

The Li & Fung case illustrates the challenges of using data and AI to improve a supply chain that spans retailers, designers, and manufacturers. The goal is to discover if its digital platform connects business goals to decisions that can actually be made.

## Background

Li & Fung began as a Hong Kong-based broker connecting fashion retailers in North America and Europe with outsourced manufacturing and supply-chain capabilities in the Far East. It expanded into other areas and developed its own brands. Competition from online companies such as Alibaba increased the pressure to change how it served its customers. Its response was described as digital transformation, which explains the direction of its investment. However, it left business questions open such as which activities will it improve, for which customers, and how will those improvements translate into an advantage?

## What is the rationale for what they are trying to do?

As retail markets have become more disruptive and competitive, they need to pursue digital transformation to make their supply chains faster, more efficient, more adaptable, and more effective. The solutions they have designed deliver speed and efficiency, helping improve margins, reducing production lead times, and increasing speed-to-market. The solutions include virtual 3D design, dynamic costing, scenario-based analysis, forecasting, order tracking, and supply-chain visibility.

However, what I find unclear is how these capabilities will fit into their business strategy. For example, which is the priority? Is it reducing time between design and delivery, help retailers choose products, or make sourcing more reliable? Each would required different decisions and measures of success. Digital transformation describes a way to change a business, but it doesn't, by itself, explain how the business will improve.

## What are the AI elements, and what are they supposed to do?

After reading the sources, I think we can deduce that they are using AI models in their solutions. Their Trend Engine platform is likely to use market data to provide insights into best-selling products and emerging trends, while their dynamic costing tools seek to connect design with business outcomes using predictability. Their official page also mentions AI tools like scenario-based analysis, forecasting, pre-costing, and control tower systems that enable faster planning and operational decisions. Additionally, after some research, I have found that they have recently partnered with Make the Dot, an AI-powered collaborative fashion design platform, to change how creative concepts are developed and increase efficiency and visual accuracy, leading to reductions in production time while maintaining good quality.

Nevertheless, these possibilities do not show how its outputs changes the process or if their tools use AI in their everyday processes. To know by certain if they use AI models and if they provide value, I would need to see the inputs, the predictions or the recommendations produced.

## What data is needed, and how is it acquired?

For the trend engine, market and trend data is needed as an input to predict emerging trends or products. To enrich this information, they also obtain customer preferences that, after the analysis and predictions, will support design selection and buying decisions. Now, for the global collaboration and 3D design environment, product development data such as patterns, materials, or blocks is needed. For the predictions in operations, data would include supplier and factory information, costs, capacity, and production status. This data is obtained through their connected digital platform, market-intelligence activities, and supplier/factory network, with some information updated in real-time. For this reason, the heterogeneous data should be standardized and integrated to be useful for the models.

As we can see, much of the data is obtained by retailers, suppliers or factories. These could represent a challenge because these organisations would need to agree to share this information, keep it current and use compatible definitions. This will represent an issue when the data is incomplete or inconsistent. Again we can conclude that the platform could have problems delivering their proposed benefits if the data is not correctly integrated or well-defined.

## What is the interface with humans and expert operators?

Humans will remain at the centre of the process, and their performance will be increased by AI. The retail and design teams will use the global collaboration environment as a virtual workspace; they can work remotely and access different materials, blocks, patterns, and digital product development tools. Designer teams can use the platform to develop creative concepts with enhanced visual accuracy, while market-intelligence and analytics teams will use the information provided by real-time insights and predictions to adjust the product strategy.

Expert operators will have a different interface because they will have a more operational relationship with the system. Merchandisers can use the dynamic cost, forecasting, and scenario-based models to connect design with business decisions, while supply-chain and production teams can use order tracking, quality-resource optimization, and control-tower visibility to manage execution and performance.

As we can see, AI will not automate everything in the process but will help to deliver faster outcomes, while humans will still use the data to analyse the context and make decisions with it. This means that the value of the platform will not only depend on collecting and processing the data, but also on delivering the right information to the different users so they can use it effectively.

However, we can also note that, even with these definitions, the digital transformation proposal do not have defined information on how the business strategy will be met and how the different roles will act upon some actions. For example, how will these users see uncertainties or correct inaccurate information, who exactly will review a forecast before it changes an order, or which will be the action plan when a recommendation and the actual situation of a factory are conflicting with each other.

## Conclusion

The main idea of Li & Fung could be in the right path, but it needs more connection with the business objective. It must have accesible data, defined analytical task, and a person who acts in the result. Until these connections are clear, digital transformation for Li & Fung will remain as an ambition rathen than a business strategy.
