# Taxonomy and human in the loop

The use-case taxonomy provides an understanding of the various ways automation and machine learning can be integrated into an organization's processes. These applications range from solutions based on predefined rules, where the system executes actions in response to specific conditions, to more advanced uses where machine learning models enable the automation of decisions, the monitoring of behaviors, anomaly detection, or the anticipation of events based on large volumes of data.

Personalization and optimization also fall within this classification. The former seeks to tailor products, services, or experiences to the characteristics and needs of each user, while the latter focuses on identifying the best possible alternative by taking objectives and constraints into account. Together, these categories help identify not only which tasks can be automated but also how data can be leveraged to improve decision-making and generate greater value across various contexts.

To illustrate it, I will analize some business cases that can use ML to improve their results:

## Software services: Implementing low-code or no-code services to build out applications

### Which of the use cases in the taxonomy (Rule-based automation, ML-enabled automation, ML-enabled monitoring and sensing, personalization, or optimization), makes the most sense for this scenario?

Rule-based automation because this kind of platform automates repetitive software development activities using predefined components or templates to enable the user to create buttons, forms, tables, etc. in a user-friendly way with faster results. For more advanced platforms, it could also be ML-enabled automation, because ML or generative AI could be used to suggest workflows, generate code or interfaces, identify errors, and recommend application components based on user requirements. Nowadays, this kind of help is known as vibe-coding, because you use the user requirements and ask an AI to build an entire module or functionality from those.

### How would human experts engage with this scenario?

The human experts in this scenario are the software developers, and they would interact in two ways with the solution. When referring to rule-based automation, they will interact with the platform and decide which components are needed, how they will be ordered, and which functionality they will cover. They also need to define the specific requirements, decide the architecture, define the security standards, and develop the integrations with external platforms. For the case of ML-enabled automation, apart from the already mentioned requirements for the rule-based automation case, they would need to review the code of the generated applications or modules to determine if the solution meets performance, security, and maintainability requirements to be put in production. This is especially important for complex or high-risk applications because any problem in the platform that occurs in production would stop the entire operation and the business will be impacted economically.

## Accounting: Automation for tasks such as payroll processing, review and reconcile transactions, record and categorize expenses, and invoice clients

### Which of the use cases in the taxonomy (Rule-based automation, ML-enabled automation, ML-enabled monitoring and sensing, personalization, or optimization), makes the most sense for this scenario?

For an initial development, rule-based automation makes the most sense because tasks in accounting are repetitive and governed by clear rules, making the input and the output structured. That includes all the tasks mentioned: payroll processing, scheduled invoices, transaction matching, and expense categorization. Furthermore, the process could be improved by using ML-enabled automation by training a model with the data to do more complex tasks like recognizing transaction patterns, categorizing unfamiliar expenses, identifying possible duplicates, and flagging anomaly transactions. Every one of these results would require further investigation and approval of an expert in the area.

### How would human experts engage with this scenario?

The experts on this scenario are the accountants, who normally would have to deal with doing all the tasks manually or using some formulas in Excel. For the rule-based automation system to work correctly, the accountants should participate in the development process, giving insights into which rules are required for the tasks and which exceptions exist for the operations in the business. When the ML-ruled scenario began its development, they would still be in charge of verifying the unusual transactions detected and approving important financial decisions that require an expert touch. They would stop doing repetitive tasks and begin to focus more on financial analysis, compliance, and cases requiring professional judgement.

## Restaurant operations: Automating everything from order placing to end-product assembly

### Which of the use cases in the taxonomy (Rule-based automation, ML-enabled automation, ML-enabled monitoring and sensing, personalization, or optimization), makes the most sense for this scenario?

This is a very interesting scenario, because if we want to cover all the operations, we could use many of the use cases presented in the videos; it would depend on the objective and nature of the business to choose which one to use. As many restaurant activities follow a set of predictable steps, rule-based automation becomes highly relevant. The customer takes an order through a web page or a mobile app, which will be received by the respective preparation station to begin the cooking process while the app calculates the total price and updates the order status for the customer. If the restaurant is using automated kitchens, we enter the use case of ML-enabled monitoring and sensing combined with rule-based automation, because you can use the machines to follow programmed instructions based on a recipe (rule-based), but at the same time, these machines have sensors that monitor temperature, humidity, time, and other variables to assemble the products.

ML-enabled automation can also be used to make the processes more adaptive to information such as order volume, preparation times, day of the week, weather, or special events to predict how busy the restaurant will be and automatically adjust the staffing recommendations or the ingredient availability. Optimization becomes valuable when thinking about the specific objectives of the restaurants and how they need to balance the reduction of the customer waiting line, the minimization of food waste, the maintenance of sufficient inventory, and the efficiency of the employees and kitchen equipment. By balancing all this information, the system must find the most efficient order-preparation sequence, which will be done in parallel, and which should wait a little longer to begin. Finally, personalization would play a role when analysing the previous purchases of the customer to recommend meals or modifications relevant to a particular customer. For example, for a customer who always orders vegetarian meals, the restaurant could show recommendations on vegetarian plates.

### How would human experts engage with this scenario?

The human experts who will participate in the process are restaurant managers, chefs, food-safety specialists, and employees. They will first be part of designing the system, defining the rules it will follow, and determining which parts of the process will be fully automated and which still need to be assessed by a professional. Chefs would focus on recipes, portions, preparation sequences, and quality standards. Food-safety professionals would establish requirements for handling ingredients and preventing cross-contamination.

When the system is up, chefs and employees would still be needed to supervise the system, ensuring the quality of the food, and manage exceptions such as equipment failure, unavailable ingredients, unusual modification requests, or customers with a severe allergy to some ingredients. They would also be interacting with the customers directly, responding to complaints, and explaining menu options to them. Finally, managers will review the recommendations of the system to make strategic decisions. Even if the model could predict a low demand, the managers are the ones who have the complete contextual information at a given moment (spontaneous events, local celebrations, etc) and could decide if that will be the case or if the system would need to produce the recommended amount of food or not.
